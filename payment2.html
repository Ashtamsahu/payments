1
What is a Credit Card?

A credit card is a financial instrument that allows you to borrow money from the card's issuing bank up to a pre-approved limit, to pay for purchases. You repay the bank later — either in full or in installments.

💳 How It Works

  • Bank lends you money to make purchases
  • You get a monthly statement with due date
  • Pay in full → no interest charged
  • Pay partially → interest applies on balance
  • Interest rates typically 18–36% per year

✅ Key Benefits

  • Builds credit history & score
  • Strong fraud protection (disputes)
  • Reward points, cashback, miles
  • Accepted globally via networks
  • Emergency purchasing power
Simple Example

You buy a $500 laptop with your credit card. Your bank pays the merchant $500. You now owe the bank $500. If you pay by your due date, no interest. If you pay only $100, interest accrues on the remaining $400.

Credit System
Why Banks Trust You
Before approving a card or setting a credit limit, banks evaluate your financial trustworthiness through credit scores and history.
1
Credit Limit

The credit limit is the maximum amount you can borrow on your credit card. Banks set it based on multiple factors.

💰 Income

Higher income → bank is comfortable extending more credit. Income determines repayment ability.

📊 Credit Score

Higher score = proven track record = higher limit offered. Below 600 = likely only secured card.

📋 Credit History

Past payment behavior, how long you've had credit, any defaults or late payments.

📉 Debt-to-Income

If you already carry heavy loan obligations, the bank may limit new credit exposure.

⚖️ Utilization

Using <30% of your limit is ideal. High utilization signals financial stress to lenders.

🏦 Bank Relationship

Existing account holders with good standing often get better limits on new cards.

4
Credit Bureaus

Credit bureaus are agencies that collect and maintain your credit history. Lenders report to them; they calculate your score.

Equifax

Founded 1899. Atlanta, GA. Uses FICO 5 model. One of the oldest credit agencies globally.

Experian

Global presence. Costa Mesa, CA. Uses FICO 2 model. Largest bureau by global reach.

TransUnion

Chicago, IL. Uses FICO 4 model. Also used for employment & tenant screening.

India Note

India has 4 RBI-licensed bureaus: CIBIL (most used), Experian India, Equifax India, and CRIF High Mark. Banks typically check CIBIL first.

5
How Lenders Report Data
1
New Account Opened When you open a credit card, auto loan, home loan — the lender reports account details to all bureaus within 30–45 days.
2
Monthly Reporting (End of Every Month) Every lender sends your: current balance, credit limit, payment status (current / 30 days late / 60 days late / default), EMI amount. This is the heartbeat of your credit file.
3
Bureau Updates Your Score After receiving monthly reports, bureaus recalculate your score. Scores can move up or down month to month based on your behavior.
4
New Lender Pulls Your Report (Hard Inquiry) When you apply for new credit, the lender does a hard inquiry which temporarily lowers your score by ~5–10 points. Multiple inquiries in 30 days signal financial distress.
✅ Good Payment

Paid on time → bureau records "Current." Positive marks stay for 10 years.

❌ Late / Missed Payment

30+ days late → negative mark filed. Stays on your report for 7 years in US, 3 years in India.

Payment Ecosystem
Who Is Involved?
Every payment involves multiple parties. Understanding each role prevents confusion when diagnosing transaction failures.
1
Payment Gateway — Authorize.net

A payment gateway is the technology layer that connects your website or application to the banking system. It securely captures, encrypts, and transmits card data.

  • Encrypts card data (SSL/TLS)
  • Sends transaction to payment processor
  • Returns approval/decline code to merchant
  • Handles fraud detection
  • Manages tokenization (replaces card data)
Analogy

The gateway is like the POS terminal / cash register at a store. It handles the "handshake" between customer and bank.

3
Issuing Bank (Cardholder's Bank)

The bank that issues the credit or debit card to the customer. Takes on the credit risk and manages the cardholder relationship.

  • Issues the physical or virtual card
  • Sets the credit limit
  • Approves or declines transactions
  • Holds or releases authorized funds
  • Handles cardholder fraud disputes

Examples

Chase Bank, Citi, Bank of America, Wells Fargo (US)

HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, SBI, Axis Bank (India)

4
Acquiring Bank (Merchant Bank)

The bank that processes payments on behalf of the merchant (the business). Receives funds from issuing bank and deposits into merchant's account.

  • Provides the merchant account
  • Receives settled funds from issuing bank
  • Deposits net amount to merchant
  • Charges merchant processing fees
  • Handles chargebacks from merchant side

Examples

Wells Fargo Merchant Services, First Data, Chase Paymentech (US)

Stripe, Square, Razorpay (act as merchant bank + gateway)

Transaction Data
What Information Is Required?
Every payment requires specific data fields. Getting these wrong causes declines and AVS failures.
1
Credit Card Details Required
Card Number
16-digit Primary Account Number (PAN)

Unique to each card. First 6 digits = BIN (identifies bank & network). Never store raw card numbers — use tokenization.

Expiry Date
Month and Year (MM/YYYY)

Card valid through the last day of that month. Gateways auto-reject expired cards. Format: 12/2028.

CVV Code
Card Verification Value — 3 or 4 digits

Back of card for Visa/Mastercard (3 digits). Front of card for AmEx (4 digits). Must not be stored after authorization per PCI DSS.

Billing Address
Address registered with the issuing bank

Used for AVS (Address Verification System). Mismatches trigger soft declines. Must match exactly what the bank has on file.

Core Transaction Flow
Authentication, Authorization & Settlement
The most important section. These concepts apply to every single transaction.
1
Authentication vs Authorization

🔐 Authentication — "Who Are You?"

Verifying the cardholder's identity. Ensures the person making the payment is the legitimate cardholder.

  • CVV verification
  • 3D Secure (OTP, biometric)
  • Verified by Visa / Mastercard SecureCode
  • PIN entry (chip cards)

✅ Authorization — "Can They Pay?"

Checking with the issuing bank that the cardholder has sufficient funds/credit and the transaction is approved.

  • Bank checks available balance/credit
  • Checks for fraud flags
  • Approves or declines
  • Places a hold on the authorized amount
Key Order

Authentication happens first (who are you?), then Authorization (do you have the money?). A transaction can be authenticated but still declined for insufficient funds.

2
Authorization vs Settlement

🔒 Authorization — Temporary Hold

Bank says "Yes, approved." Money is reserved but NOT transferred.

  • Happens in ~2 seconds
  • Customer sees "Pending" on statement
  • Hold expires in ~7 days if not captured
  • Merchant receives authorization code
  • No money has actually moved

✅ Settlement — Actual Transfer

The actual movement of funds from issuing bank to merchant's acquiring bank.

  • Happens 1–3 business days after capture
  • Charge becomes permanent on statement
  • Batched and processed (usually end of day)
  • Fees are deducted at this stage
  • Funds deposited to merchant account

Transaction Types
Real Business Logic
Different business scenarios require different transaction types. These are all available in Authorize.net.
🧠 Mental Model — Remember This
Authorize
"Do they have the money?"
→
Capture
"Charge them now"
→
Settle
"Move money to merchant"
Type 01

Auth Only

Checks funds and places a hold only. No capture → no money collected.

Use CaseHotels, car rentals, gas stations pre-auth
Type 02

Auth + Capture (Delayed)

Authorize first → capture later when ready. Two separate steps.

Use CaseRestaurants (tip added later), e-commerce (charge on ship)
Type 03

Sale (Immediate)

Auth and capture happen instantly in one step. Most common type.

Use CaseRetail checkout, most online purchases, standard billing
Type 04

Pre-Authorization

Auth with flexibility to adjust the amount up or down before capture.

Use CaseHotels (final bill differs from check-in estimate), fuel pumps
Type 05

Partial Capture

Capture less than the authorized amount. Remaining hold released.

Use CaseOut-of-stock items: auth $200, capture only $150
Type 06

Multiple Capture

One authorization → multiple captures over time.

Use CaseSplit shipments: capture portions as each ships
Type 07

Void

Cancels an auth or uncaptured transaction. Hold disappears, no settlement.

Use CaseCustomer cancels order before capture / correct a mistake
Type 08

Refund (Post-Settlement)

Returns money after settlement. Creates new reverse transaction.

Use CaseCustomer returns item after payment already settled
⚠️ Void vs Refund — Critical Difference

Void = before settlement. Auth cancelled, hold removed, no money moved.
Refund = after settlement. Money already transferred to merchant, then returned to customer's card (takes 3–5 business days).

Test Mode vs Live Mode
🧪 TEST MODE (Sandbox)

Development Environment

No real money moves. Use for building, testing, and debugging.

  • URL: sandbox.authorize.net
  • Test card: 4111 1111 1111 1111
  • Any future expiry date
  • Any 3-digit CVV
  • Zero real charges
🟢 LIVE MODE (Production)

Production Environment

Real transactions. Real money. All charges are final.

  • URL: merchant.authorize.net
  • Real card numbers required
  • Funds actually charged
  • Separate API credentials
  • Appears on cardholder statement
⚠️ How to Switch Modes

Change the API Login ID and Transaction Key from sandbox to live credentials in your integration. Also update the API endpoint URL. Never use live credentials in a development or test environment.
